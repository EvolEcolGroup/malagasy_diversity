# Admixture and natural selection among the people of Madagascar


Our project will examine the history of genetic admixture in the Malagasy, specifically considering how assortative mating and natural selection have contributed to current patterns of both phenotypic and genetic diversity. 

#Context
The population of Madagascar exhibits a globally unique combination of African and Asian ancestries.  Malagasy African ancestry can be primarily attributed to Bantu sources, while Asian ancestry is derived from island southeast Asia (Pierron et al., 2014). Genetic admixture between these populations is estimated to have occurred between 500 and 900 years before present when Bantu populations encountered Austronesian communities (Pierron et al., 2014; 2017; Brucato et al., 2016). However, given limited genetic sampling of the Malagasy, elements of this admixture process remain unresolved. The precise timing of admixture, and whether multiple migrations contributed to the admixture observed among the Malagasy today, are still subject to debate. Malagasy histories also refer to native populations, named the Vazimba (Tofanelli and Bertoncini, 2010), who occupied the island prior to Austronesian and Bantu arrivals. So far, no genetic evidence has identified any contribution to modern Malagasy individuals from such pre-Bantu African sources (Pierron et al., 2014). However, given recent archaeological evidence extending the time depth of human activity on Madagascar to the early Holocene (Hansford et al., 2018), alongside the greater availability of comparative genetic data from Africa, both ancient and modern, this possibility merits re-examination.
